#+TITLE: Globe lineation: the witness-page pipeline
#+DATE: 2026-10-07
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

* What this is

=src/globe/= regenerates a play's Globe Shakespeare line numbering from
page images of the printed Globe, and writes the play's Globe edition
for canonical-engLit. It was developed in =globe-lineation-workshop=
and moved here unchanged except in imports and paths; the workshop is
frozen at its tag =v1.0= as the record of how it was built (see
[[*References to the workshop]]).

King Lear is built and published (canonical-engLit =mvp=,
=data/shakespeare/lr/shakespeare.lr.globe.xml=). Antony is next. The
open tasks are in canonical-engLit =doc/agenda.org= on =mvp=.

* The method

Words and structure come from the P4 (=Renaissance/Shakespeare/opensource/=
in canonical-engLit, read-only), converted to P5 by =p4_convert.py=.
Line starts come from the page:

1. Each printed page is read from a witness's Kraken ALTO
   (=page_rows.py=). Rows are classified by their offset from the
   column margin, in units of row pitch: start, turnover, indented,
   displaced. The page's own marginal numbers are taken off and
   attached to the last row of their line.
2. The rows are aligned to the P4's words (=align_play.py=,
   =tokens.py=), so every row knows the word it starts at.
3. A Globe line is a start-band row carrying speech plus every turnover
   or displaced row after it (=lineate.py=). Lines are counted from
   each scene's start, across page breaks.
4. The P5 is written with a =<milestone unit="line" ed="Globe" n=…/>=
   at the start of every Globe line and an =<lb/>= at every other
   printed row (=emit_tei.py=), under the existing P5's header,
   rewritten (=tei_header.py=).

Two witnesses, both 1,054 leaves, OCR'd whole: =miun= (Michigan, 1866)
and =trent= (Trent, 1887). They share plates, so either is authoritative
for line breaks. Page by page, =miun= is tried first and =trent= if
=miun= does not close (=regenerate.ORDER=). Their provenance is in
=doc/witnesses.org=.

* The gate

The printed marginal numbers number nothing; they are the check. Every
printed number must equal the count of its line, counted from the scene
start. If any page fails, the play is not written; the reports are, so
the failures can be read. Lear: 32/32 pages, 0 failing intervals,
3,332 Globe lines.

Regeneration moves no words: the output's word stream must equal the
P4's, or the build stops.

* The tables (=data/globe/=)

| Table                  | What it says                                                     | Who changes it                       |
|------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| =witnesses.toml=       | where each witness's scans and OCR layers are, under =GLOBE_WITNESSES= | when a witness is added or moved |
| =leaves-miun.tsv=, =leaves-trent.tsv= | leaf -> printed page, with the basis of each row  | reviewed; built by the workshop's =build_leaf_tables.py= |
| =witness-offsets.tsv=  | the running-head audit's offsets per witness; the source of the leaf tables, not read by the build | as above |
| =shared-lines.tsv=     | junctions the page cannot show, and numerals both witnesses misread | rows proposed by the build or from the Folger; =checked= is filled by Cliff, after looking at the page image |
| =witness-manifest.tsv= | sha256 of every witness file the build reads, per play           | =globe-lineation manifest PLAY=, deliberately |

*=checked= is Cliff's.* The canonical build refuses to use a
=shared-lines.tsv= row whose =checked= is empty; the review build is
still written (=--allow-pending= overrides, for development only).

*The manifest pins the witnesses.* The build refuses a leaf (or leaf
table) that is not in =witness-manifest.tsv= or whose sha256 differs.
Rewriting a play's rows is how a deliberate change to a witness is
recorded.

* The two builds and the reports

- Canonical: =out/globe/PLAY/shakespeare.PLAY.globe.xml=. Not tracked
  here: its home is canonical-engLit, where it is copied to publish.
- Review: =shakespeare.PLAY.globe.review.xml=, the canonical with XML
  comments for a reader checking it against the page: each page's
  witness and leaf, where one witness was taken over the other, each
  =shared-lines.tsv= row applied, and the rows the reports flag (word
  disagreements, P4 anchors, verse/prose, borderline rows). Tracked.
  Stripping the comments must give the canonical bytes, or the build
  stops.
- Reports: =reports/globe/regenerate-PLAY-*.tsv= (gate, intervals, word
  disagreements, old-vs-new P4 anchors, verse/prose, borderline rows,
  Folger check, @part against the page), and the Schmidt smoke test,
  =schmidt-smoke-lr.{tsv,md}=, which records the edition's sha256.

Each build carries a stamp in =<revisionDesc>=: the tool, its version,
the corpus-tools commit, and the sha256 of the P4, =shared-lines.tsv=
and the shell. A published edition is the shell of its next build, and
its earlier regeneration stamp is replaced, not added to.

* Running it

Inputs, as siblings of this repository:
- =../canonical-engLit=, with =mvp= checked out (the build refuses
  otherwise: the P4 and the shell are read from its working tree);
- the witnesses: the =PerseusDLCode/globe-witnesses= repository, at
  =$GLOBE_WITNESSES= (default =../globe-witnesses=; =doc/witnesses.org=);
- =../schmidt-lexicon-workshop/out/= for the smoke test;
- the Folger (DraCor) Lear at the absolute path in =shared_lines.FOLGER=,
  for the Folger report only; skipped if absent.

#+begin_src sh
make globe-verify PLAY=lr      # build to a temp dir; equal to the published file, stamp aside?
make globe-regenerate PLAY=lr  # write out/globe/lr and reports/globe; refuses on a dirty tree
.venv/bin/globe-lineation smoke          # Schmidt's Lear citations against out/globe/lr
.venv/bin/globe-lineation manifest lr    # re-pin the witness files the build reads
.venv/bin/globe-lineation regenerate lr --scratch=DIR   # a development build, anywhere
#+end_src

*Verify* is the fixed-point check: built from the published file as its
shell, the play must come back byte for byte, except the stamp's
=<change>= (its date, commit and shell sha256).

*Regenerate refuses a dirty tree* rather than stamp a commit that does
not describe the code. A rebuild that changes nothing still changes the
review build's stamp, so commit or discard it.

Serialisation is what byte identity tests: lxml is locked at 6.1.3
(libxml2 2.14.6), as it was in the workshop.

* Adding a play

=regenerate.PLAYS= has one entry, Lear: the P4 path, the P5 shell and
the first and last printed page. A play also needs its rows in
=witness-manifest.tsv=, and its shared lines reviewed into
=shared-lines.tsv=. The per-play table that should replace =PLAYS= and
=shared_lines.FOLGER= is the first task carried to canonical-engLit.

* References to the workshop

The code and tests cite the workshop's deliberation in the form
=doc/agenda.org #id= and =doc/forum.org #id=, and some of its reports
(=reports/doubleday-comparison.md=, the page-alignment and baseline-merge
reports). These are to =globe-lineation-workshop= at its tag =v1.0= (the
move was made from its commit =9aeea3a=; nothing in the code changed
after), not to files in this repository. Likewise =CLAUDE.md= in a code comment is
the workshop's. =spikes/2026-09-21-regeneration= and the witness
preparation tools (OCR, binarisation, ALTO pruning, the leaf-table
builder and the running-head audit) stayed there.

=p4_convert.py= is this repository's own =src/globe_lineation.py= at
=10a6f74= (P4->P5 conversion only), which the workshop vendored; the
original is under the tag =archive/globe-rederive=.
